## 第一篇：First Principles: Elon Musk on the Power of Thinking for Yourself

### Article Information｜文章信息

- **Author｜作者：** [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/about)
- **Original article｜原文链接：** [https://jamesclear.com/first-principles](https://jamesclear.com/first-principles)
- **Tags｜标签：** first principles · mental model · innovation · thinking

---

First principles thinking, which is sometimes called reasoning from first principles, is one of the most effective strategies you can employ for breaking down complicated problems and generating original solutions. It also might be the single best approach to learn how to think for yourself.

> **中文翻译**
第一性原理思考，有时也被称为从第一性原理出发进行推理，是拆解复杂问题并生成原创解决方案时最有效的策略之一。它也可能是学习如何独立思考的最佳方法。
>

The first principles approach has been used by many great thinkers including inventor Johannes Gutenberg, military strategist John Boyd, and the ancient philosopher Aristotle, but no one embodies the philosophy of first principles thinking more effectively than entrepreneur Elon Musk.

> **中文翻译**
许多伟大的思想家都使用过第一性原理方法，其中包括发明家约翰内斯·古登堡、军事战略家约翰·博伊德和古代哲学家亚里士多德；但没有谁比企业家埃隆·马斯克更充分地体现第一性原理思考的哲学。
>

In 2002, Musk began his quest to send the first rocket to Mars—an idea that would eventually become the aerospace company SpaceX.

> **中文翻译**
2002 年，马斯克开始探索把第一枚火箭送往火星——这个想法后来发展成了航空航天公司 SpaceX。
>

He ran into a major challenge right off the bat. After visiting a number of aerospace manufacturers around the world, Musk discovered the cost of purchasing a rocket was astronomical—up to \$65 million. Given the high price, he began to rethink the problem.[1]

> **中文翻译**
他一开始就遇到了一个重大挑战。在走访世界各地多家航空航天制造商后，马斯克发现购买一枚火箭的价格高得惊人——最高可达 6500 万美元。面对如此高昂的价格，他开始重新思考这个问题。
>

“I tend to approach things from a physics framework,” Musk said in an interview. “Physics teaches you to reason from first principles rather than by analogy. So I said, okay, let’s look at the first principles. What is a rocket made of? Aerospace-grade aluminum alloys, plus some titanium, copper, and carbon fiber. Then I asked, what is the value of those materials on the commodity market? It turned out that the materials cost of a rocket was around two percent of the typical price.”[2]

> **中文翻译**
马斯克在一次采访中说：“我倾向于从物理学的框架出发处理问题。物理学教你从第一性原理而不是类比出发进行推理。所以我说，好吧，让我们看看第一性原理。火箭由什么制成？航空级铝合金，再加上一些钛、铜和碳纤维。然后我问，这些材料在大宗商品市场上价值多少？结果发现，火箭的材料成本大约只占通常售价的百分之二。”
>

Instead of buying a finished rocket for tens of millions, Musk decided to create his own company, purchase the raw materials for cheap, and build the rockets himself. SpaceX was born.

> **中文翻译**
马斯克没有花费数千万美元购买成品火箭，而是决定创办自己的公司，以低价购买原材料并自行制造火箭。SpaceX 就此诞生。
>

Within a few years, SpaceX had cut the price of launching a rocket by nearly 10x while still making a profit. Musk used first principles thinking to break the situation down to the fundamentals, bypass the high prices of the aerospace industry, and create a more effective solution.[3]

> **中文翻译**
几年之内，SpaceX 在仍然盈利的同时，将火箭发射价格降低了将近十倍。马斯克运用第一性原理思考，把局面拆解到最基本的层面，绕过航空航天行业的高昂价格，创造出一个更有效的解决方案。
>

First principles thinking is the act of boiling a process down to the fundamental parts that you know are true and building up from there. Let's discuss how you can utilize first principles thinking in your life and work.

> **中文翻译**
第一性原理思考，就是把一个过程逐步还原为那些你确定为真的基本组成部分，然后从那里重新向上构建。下面我们来讨论如何在生活和工作中运用第一性原理思考。
>

### Defining First Principles Thinking｜定义第一性原理思考

A first principle is a basic assumption that cannot be deduced any further. Over two thousand years ago, Aristotle defined a first principle as “the first basis from which a thing is known.”[4]

> **中文翻译**
第一性原理是一项无法再进一步推导的基本前提。两千多年前，亚里士多德把第一性原理定义为“认识一件事物所依据的最初基础”。
>

First principles thinking is a fancy way of saying “think like a scientist.” Scientists don’t assume anything. They start with questions like, *What are we absolutely sure is true? What has been proven?*

> **中文翻译**
第一性原理思考，是“像科学家一样思考”的一种听起来更高级的说法。科学家不会预设任何事情。他们会从这样的问题开始：*哪些事情是我们绝对确定为真的？哪些事情已经得到了证明？*
>

In theory, first principles thinking requires you to dig deeper and deeper until you are left with only the foundational truths of a situation. Rene Descartes, the French philosopher and scientist, embraced this approach with a method now called Cartesian Doubt in which he would “systematically doubt everything he could possibly doubt until he was left with what he saw as purely indubitable truths.”[5]

> **中文翻译**
从理论上说，第一性原理思考要求你一层又一层地深入挖掘，直到某个情境中只剩下基础事实。法国哲学家兼科学家勒内·笛卡尔采用了这种方法；如今它被称为“笛卡尔式怀疑”。他会“系统地怀疑一切可能怀疑的事物，直到只剩下他认为完全不容置疑的真理”。
>

In practice, you don't have to simplify every problem down to the atomic level to get the benefits of first principles thinking. You just need to go one or two levels deeper than most people. Different solutions present themselves at different layers of abstraction. John Boyd, the famous fighter pilot and military strategist, created the following thought experiment which showcases how to use first principles thinking in a practical way.[6]

> **中文翻译**
在实践中，你不必把每个问题都简化到原子层面，也能从第一性原理思考中获益。你只需要比大多数人多深入一两层。不同的解决方案会在不同的抽象层次上显现出来。著名战斗机飞行员和军事战略家约翰·博伊德设计了下面这个思想实验，展示如何以实用的方式运用第一性原理思考。
>

Imagine you have three things:

> **中文翻译**
想象一下，你拥有三样东西：
>
- A motorboat with a skier behind it
- A military tank
- A bicycle

> **中文翻译**
>
> - 一艘后面拖着滑水者的摩托艇
> - 一辆军用坦克
> - 一辆自行车

Now, let's break these items down into their constituent parts:

> **中文翻译**
现在，让我们把这些物品拆解成它们的组成部分：
>
- Motorboat: motor, the hull of a boat, and a pair of skis.
- Tank: metal treads, steel armor plates, and a gun.
- Bicycle: handlebars, wheels, gears, and a seat.

> **中文翻译**
>
> - 摩托艇：发动机、船体和一副滑水板。
> - 坦克：金属履带、钢制装甲板和一门火炮。
> - 自行车：车把、车轮、齿轮和座椅。

What can you create from these individual parts? One option is to make a snowmobile by combining the handlebars and seat from the bike, the metal treads from the tank, and the motor and skis from the boat.

> **中文翻译**
你能用这些独立部件创造出什么？一种选择是：把自行车的车把和座椅、坦克的金属履带，以及摩托艇的发动机和滑水板组合起来，制造一辆雪地摩托。
>

This is the process of first principles thinking in a nutshell. It is a cycle of breaking a situation down into the core pieces and then putting them all back together in a more effective way. Deconstruct then reconstruct.

> **中文翻译**
简而言之，这就是第一性原理思考的过程。它是一个循环：先把一种情境拆成核心部件，再以更有效的方式把它们重新组合起来。先解构，再重构。
>

### How First Principles Drive Innovation｜第一性原理如何驱动创新

The snowmobile example also highlights another hallmark of first principles thinking, which is the combination of ideas from seemingly unrelated fields. A tank and a bicycle appear to have nothing in common, but pieces of a tank and a bicycle can be combined to develop innovations like a snowmobile.

> **中文翻译**
雪地摩托的例子也凸显了第一性原理思考的另一个特征：把看似毫不相关的领域中的想法结合起来。坦克和自行车表面上毫无共同之处，但它们的部件可以组合起来，创造出雪地摩托这样的新事物。
>

Many of the most groundbreaking ideas in history have been a result of boiling things down to the first principles and then substituting a more effective solution for one of the key parts.

> **中文翻译**
历史上许多最具突破性的想法，都是先把事物还原到第一性原理，再用一种更有效的解决方案替换其中某个关键部分的结果。
>

For instance, Johannes Gutenberg combined the technology of a screw press—a device used for making wine—with movable type, paper, and ink to create the printing press. Movable type had been used for centuries, but Gutenberg was the first person to consider the constituent parts of the process and adapt technology from an entirely different field to make printing far more efficient. The result was a world-changing innovation and the widespread distribution of information for the first time in history.[7]

> **中文翻译**
例如，约翰内斯·古登堡把螺旋压榨机——一种用于酿酒的设备——的技术与活字、纸张和油墨结合起来，创造了印刷机。活字已经沿用了几个世纪，但古登堡是第一个审视整个流程的组成部分，并借用一个完全不同领域的技术来大幅提高印刷效率的人。其结果是一项改变世界的创新，也让信息第一次在历史上得到广泛传播。
>

The best solution is not where everyone is already looking.

> **中文翻译**
最佳解决方案并不在所有人都已经盯着的地方。
>

First principles thinking helps you to cobble together information from different disciplines to create new ideas and innovations. You start by getting to the facts. Once you have a foundation of facts, you can make a plan to [improve each little piece](https://jamesclear.com/marginal-gains). This process naturally leads to exploring widely for better substitutes.

> **中文翻译**
第一性原理思考能帮助你把不同学科的信息拼接起来，创造新的想法和创新。你首先要抵达事实。在拥有事实基础之后，就可以制定计划去[改进每一个小部件](https://jamesclear.com/marginal-gains)。这个过程自然会促使你广泛探索，寻找更好的替代方案。
>

### The Challenge of Reasoning From First Principles｜从第一性原理推理的挑战

First principles thinking can be easy to describe, but quite difficult to practice. One of the primary obstacles to first principles thinking is our tendency to optimize *form* rather than *function*. The story of the suitcase provides a perfect example.

> **中文翻译**
第一性原理思考很容易描述，实践起来却相当困难。其中一个主要障碍，是我们倾向于优化*形式*而不是*功能*。旅行箱的故事提供了一个绝佳范例。
>

In ancient Rome, soldiers used leather messenger bags and satchels to carry food while riding across the countryside. At the same time, the Romans had many vehicles with wheels like chariots, carriages, and wagons. And yet, for thousands of years, nobody thought to combine the bag and the wheel. The first rolling suitcase wasn’t invented until 1970 when Bernard Sadow was hauling his luggage through an airport and saw a worker rolling a heavy machine on a wheeled skid.[8]

> **中文翻译**
在古罗马，士兵骑马穿越乡间时会用皮革信使包和挎包携带食物。与此同时，罗马人已经拥有战车、马车和货车等许多带轮子的交通工具。然而，几千年来，没有人想到把包和轮子结合起来。直到 1970 年，伯纳德·萨多在机场拖运行李时，看见一名工人用带轮滑架移动一台重型机器，第一个滚轮旅行箱才由此诞生。
>

Throughout the 1800s and 1900s, leather bags were specialized for particular uses—backpacks for school, rucksacks for hiking, suitcases for travel. Zippers were added to bags in 1938. Nylon backpacks were first sold in 1967.[9] Despite these improvements, the form of the bag remained largely the same. Innovators spent all of their time making slight iterations on the same theme.

> **中文翻译**
在整个 19 世纪和 20 世纪，皮包逐渐按特定用途分化——上学用双肩包、徒步用帆布背包、旅行用手提箱。1938 年，人们把拉链加到包上；1967 年，尼龙背包首次上市销售。 尽管有这些改进，包的形态基本没有变化。创新者把所有时间都用在对同一主题进行小幅迭代上。
>

What looks like innovation is often an iteration of previous forms rather than an improvement of the core function. While everyone else was focused on how to build a better bag (form), Sadow considered how to store and move things more efficiently (function).

> **中文翻译**
看起来像创新的东西，往往只是对既有形式的迭代，而不是对核心功能的改进。当其他人都在关注如何造一个更好的包（形式）时，萨多思考的是如何更高效地储存和移动物品（功能）。
>

### How to Think for Yourself｜如何独立思考

The human tendency for imitation is a common roadblock to first principles thinking. When most people envision the future, they project the current *form* forward rather than projecting the *function* forward and abandoning the form.

> **中文翻译**
人类的模仿倾向，是第一性原理思考常见的障碍。当大多数人设想未来时，他们会把当前的*形式*向前延伸，而不是把*功能*向前延伸并抛弃原有形式。
>

For instance, when criticizing technological progress some people ask, “Where are the flying cars?”

> **中文翻译**
例如，有些人在批评技术进步时会问：“会飞的汽车在哪里？”
>

Here's the thing: We have flying cars. They're called airplanes. People who ask this question are so focused on form (a flying object that looks like a car) that they overlook the function (transportation by flight).[10] This is what Elon Musk is referring to when he says that people often “live life by analogy.”

> **中文翻译**
事实是：我们已经有会飞的汽车了，它们叫作飞机。提出这个问题的人太专注于形式（一种看起来像汽车的飞行物），以至于忽略了功能（通过飞行实现运输）。 埃隆·马斯克所说的人们常常“依照类比生活”，指的正是这种情况。
>

Be wary of the ideas you inherit. Old conventions and previous forms are often accepted without question and, once accepted, they set a boundary around creativity.[11]

> **中文翻译**
要警惕你继承来的观念。旧惯例和过去的形式经常未经质疑就被接受；一旦被接受，它们便会给创造力划定边界。
>

This difference is one of the key distinctions between [continuous improvement](https://jamesclear.com/continuous-improvement) and first principles thinking. Continuous improvement tends to occur within the boundary set by the original vision. By comparison, first principles thinking requires you to abandon your allegiance to previous forms and put the function front and center. What are you trying to accomplish? What is the functional outcome you are looking to achieve?

> **中文翻译**
这种差异，是[持续改进](https://jamesclear.com/continuous-improvement)与第一性原理思考之间的关键区别之一。持续改进往往发生在原始愿景所划定的边界内。相比之下，第一性原理思考要求你放弃对以往形式的忠诚，把功能置于中心位置。你究竟想完成什么？你希望实现的功能性结果是什么？
>

Optimize the function. Ignore the form. This is how you [learn to think for yourself](https://jamesclear.com/feynman-mental-models).

> **中文翻译**
优化功能，忽略形式。这就是你[学习独立思考](https://jamesclear.com/feynman-mental-models)的方式。
>

### The Power of First Principles｜第一性原理的力量

Ironically, perhaps the best way to develop cutting-edge ideas is to start by breaking things down to the fundamentals. Even if you aren't trying to develop innovative ideas, understanding the first principles of your field is a smart use of your time. Without a [firm grasp of the basics](https://jamesclear.com/fundamentals), there is little chance of mastering the details that make the difference at elite levels of competition.

> **中文翻译**
颇具讽刺意味的是，发展前沿想法的最佳方式，也许恰恰是先把事物拆解到最基本的层面。即使你并不打算提出创新想法，花时间理解所在领域的第一性原理也是明智的。如果不能[牢固掌握基础](https://jamesclear.com/fundamentals)，就几乎不可能掌握那些在顶尖竞争中造成差异的细节。
>

Every innovation, including the most groundbreaking ones, requires a long period of iteration and improvement. The company at the beginning of this article, SpaceX, ran many simulations, made thousands of adjustments, and required multiple trials before they figured out how to build an affordable and reusable rocket.

> **中文翻译**
每一项创新——包括最具突破性的创新——都需要经历漫长的迭代和改进。本文开头提到的 SpaceX 进行了大量模拟、做出数千次调整并经历多轮试验，才弄清楚如何制造价格可负担且可重复使用的火箭。
>

First principles thinking does not remove the need for continuous improvement, but it does alter the direction of improvement. Without reasoning by first principles, you spend your time making small improvements to a bicycle rather than a snowmobile. First principles thinking sets you on a different trajectory.

> **中文翻译**
第一性原理思考并不会消除持续改进的必要性，但它会改变改进的方向。如果不从第一性原理出发进行推理，你会把时间花在对自行车进行小修小补上，而不是制造一辆雪地摩托。第一性原理思考会把你送上一条不同的轨道。
>

If you want to enhance an existing process or belief, continuous improvement is a great option. If you want to learn how to think for yourself, reasoning from first principles is one of the best ways to do it.

> **中文翻译**
如果你想改善一个现有流程或观念，持续改进是很好的选择。如果你想学习如何独立思考，从第一性原理出发进行推理则是最佳方法之一。
>

> **中文翻译**
感谢 Benedict Evans；他的[推文启发了这个例子](https://twitter.com/benedictevans/status/884566566313971712)。
>

**[11]** Stereotypes fall into this style of thinking. “Oh, I once knew a poor person who was dumb, so all poor people must be dumb.” And so on. Anytime we judge someone by their group status rather than their individual characteristics we are reasoning about them by analogy.

> **中文翻译**
刻板印象就属于这种思维方式。“哦，我以前认识一个很笨的穷人，所以所有穷人一定都很笨。”诸如此类。每当我们依据某人的群体身份，而不是其个人特征来评判他时，我们就是在通过类比进行推理。
>

## 第二篇：What is First Principles Thinking?

### Article Information｜文章信息

- **Author｜作者：** Farnam Street
- **Original article｜原文链接：** [https://fs.blog/first-principles/](https://fs.blog/first-principles/)
- **Tags｜标签：** first principles · mental model · thinking

---

First principles thinking is one of the best ways to discover new solutions.

> **中文翻译**
第一性原理思考是发现新解决方案的最佳方式之一。
>

Sometimes called “reasoning from first principles,” it’s a tool to help break down complicated problems by separating what we know is absolutely true from anything that is an assumption. What remains are the essentials. If you know the first principles of something, you can build the rest of your knowledge around them to produce something new.

> **中文翻译**
它有时也被称为“从第一性原理出发进行推理”，是一种帮助我们拆解复杂问题的工具：把我们确定为真的事情与所有假设分离开来。剩下的就是本质。如果你掌握了一件事物的第一性原理，就能以它们为中心构建其余知识，从而创造出新的东西。
>

While you could take this way of thinking down to an atomic level, a lot of value is gained by simply going a level or two deeper than most people. Solutions are based on what you see. Different answers reveal themselves at different levels.

> **中文翻译**
虽然你可以用这种思维方式一直深入到原子层面，但仅仅比大多数人多深入一两层，就能获得很大的价值。解决方案取决于你看见了什么；不同的答案会在不同层级上显现。
>

If I hand you a house made from Lego blocks, you know it’s possible to make a house. Thinking at the first layer, you might move a few blocks around and, in the process, slightly improve the house. Most people stop here. They are presented with something that already exists and they endeavor to make it slightly better. Going a layer deeper and breaking the Lego house into individual pieces opens the door to possibility: not only can you build a better house, you can build something entirely different.

> **中文翻译**
如果我递给你一座用乐高积木搭成的房子，你知道这些积木可以造出一座房子。在第一层进行思考时，你可能会移动几块积木，并在这个过程中稍微改善房子。大多数人会停在这里。他们面对一个已经存在的东西，并努力让它稍微变好。再深入一层，把乐高房子拆成一块块独立的积木，就会打开可能性的大门：你不仅能造一座更好的房子，还能造出完全不同的东西。
>

Everything that exists is effectively a set of Lego blocks, assembled in a certain way, that can be taken apart and reassembled. A bike is just a seat, chain, body, handlebars, etc. Breaking the bike down into its parts allows you to reassemble the parts into something new. However, you can also go deeper, melting the parts into their core metals and making a shield, sword, or anything else, limited only by material and imagination.

> **中文翻译**
一切存在的事物，实际上都像是一组以特定方式组装起来的乐高积木，可以被拆开并重新组装。自行车不过是座椅、链条、车架、车把等部件。把自行车拆成零件，你就可以把这些零件重新组合成新的东西。不过，你还可以继续深入，把零件熔化还原为构成它们的金属，再制造盾牌、剑或其他任何东西；此时唯一的限制是材料和想象力。
>

> “I don’t know what’s the matter with people: they don’t learn by understanding; they learn by some other way—by rote or something. Their knowledge is so fragile!”
>
>
> — Richard Feynman
>

> **中文翻译**
“我不知道人们究竟怎么了：他们不是通过理解来学习，而是通过别的方式——死记硬背之类的方式。他们的知识如此脆弱！”
>
>
> ——理查德·费曼
>

### The Basics｜基础

The idea of building knowledge from first principles has a long tradition in philosophy. In the Western canon, it goes back to Plato, with significant contributions from Aristotle and Descartes. Essentially, these thinkers sought foundational knowledge that would remain constant and serve as a basis for building everything else, from our ethical systems to our social structures.

> **中文翻译**
从第一性原理出发构建知识，在哲学中有着悠久传统。在西方经典中，这一思想可以追溯到柏拉图，亚里士多德和笛卡尔也作出了重要贡献。从本质上说，这些思想家试图寻找能够保持不变的基础知识，并以此为基础构建其他一切——从伦理体系到社会结构。
>

First principles thinking doesn’t have to be quite so grand. When we do it, we aren’t necessarily looking for absolute truths—millennia of epistemological inquiry have shown us that these are hard to come by, and the scientific method has demonstrated that knowledge can be built only when we are actively trying to falsify it. Rather, first principles thinking identifies the elements that are, in the context of any given situation, irreducible.

> **中文翻译**
第一性原理思考不必如此宏大。当我们运用它时，并不一定是在寻找绝对真理——数千年的认识论探索已经告诉我们，绝对真理很难获得；科学方法也证明，只有当我们主动尝试证伪知识时，知识才可能被建立起来。第一性原理思考真正要做的，是识别在某个特定情境中无法再被约简的要素。
>

First principles do not provide a checklist of things that will always be true; our understanding of first principles evolves as we gain more knowledge. They are the foundation on which we must build, and thus will be different in every situation—but the more we know, the more we can challenge. For example, if we are considering how to improve the energy efficiency of a refrigerator, the laws of thermodynamics can be taken as first principles.

> **中文翻译**
第一性原理并不会提供一份永远为真的事项清单；随着我们获得更多知识，我们对第一性原理的理解也会演化。它们是我们必须在其上进行构建的基础，因此在不同情境中会有所不同——但我们知道得越多，就越能对这些基础提出挑战。例如，当我们考虑如何提高冰箱的能源效率时，可以把热力学定律视为第一性原理。
>

However, a theoretical chemist or physicist might want to explore entropy, and thus further break the second law of thermodynamics into its underlying principles and the assumptions that were made because of them.

> **中文翻译**
然而，理论化学家或物理学家可能想研究熵，因此会进一步把热力学第二定律拆解成其底层原理，以及由这些原理引出的假设。
>

> “To understand is to know what to do.”
>
>
> — Wittgenstein
>

> **中文翻译**
“理解，就是知道该做什么。”
>
>
> ——维特根斯坦
>

### Techniques for Establishing First Principles｜确立第一性原理的技巧

If we never learn to take something apart, test our assumptions about it, and reconstruct it, we end up bound by what other people tell us is possible. We end up trapped in the way things have always been done. When the environment changes, we just continue as if things were the same, making costly mistakes along the way.

> **中文翻译**
如果我们从未学会拆开一件事物、检验关于它的假设并将其重构，最终就会被别人告诉我们的“可能性”所束缚。我们会被困在“一直以来都是这样做的”这种方式里。当环境发生变化时，我们仍会假装一切没有改变，并在途中犯下代价高昂的错误。
>

Some of us are naturally skeptical of what we’re told: Maybe it doesn’t match up to our experiences. Maybe it’s something that used to be true but isn’t true anymore. Or maybe we just think differently about something. When it comes down to it, everything that is not a law of nature is just a shared belief. Money is a shared belief. So is a border. So is Bitcoin. So is love. The list goes on.

> **中文翻译**
我们中的一些人天生会怀疑别人告诉我们的事情：也许它与我们的经历不符；也许它过去是真的，如今却不再成立；又或者，我们只是从不同角度看待某件事。归根结底，凡是不属于自然定律的东西，都只是一种共享信念。货币是一种共享信念，边界也是，比特币也是，爱情也是。这样的例子还可以一直列下去。
>

There are two techniques we can use to change the level where we are looking at a situation, identify the first principles, and cut through the dogma and shared belief: Socratic questioning and the Five Whys.

> **中文翻译**
我们可以使用两种技巧来改变观察情境的层级、识别第一性原理，并穿透教条和共享信念：苏格拉底式提问，以及“五个为什么”。
>

#### Socratic Questioning｜苏格拉底式提问

Socratic questioning can be used to establish first principles through stringent analysis. This a disciplined questioning process, used to establish truths, reveal underlying assumptions, and separate knowledge from ignorance. The key distinction between Socratic questioning and normal discussions is that the former seeks to draw out first principles in a systematic manner. Socratic questioning generally follows this process:

> **中文翻译**
苏格拉底式提问可以通过严格分析来确立第一性原理。这是一套有纪律的提问过程，用于确立真相、揭示底层假设，并把知识与无知区分开来。苏格拉底式提问与普通讨论的关键区别在于，前者试图以系统化方式引出第一性原理。苏格拉底式提问通常遵循以下过程：
>
1. Clarifying your thinking and explaining the origins of your ideas (Why do I think this? What exactly do I think?)
2. Challenging assumptions (How do I know this is true? What if I thought the opposite?)
3. Looking for evidence (How can I back this up? What are the sources?)
4. Considering alternative perspectives (What might others think? How do I know I am correct?)
5. Examining consequences and implications (What if I am wrong? What are the consequences if I am?)
6. Questioning the original questions (Why did I think that? Was I correct? What conclusions can I draw from the reasoning process?)

> **中文翻译**
>
> 1. 澄清自己的思路，并解释想法的来源（我为什么这样想？我具体在想什么？）
> 2. 挑战假设（我怎么知道这是真的？如果我持相反看法会怎样？）
> 3. 寻找证据（我怎样证明它？信息来源是什么？）
> 4. 考虑其他视角（别人可能会怎么想？我怎么知道自己是正确的？）
> 5. 审视后果与影响（如果我错了会怎样？如果我错了，会产生什么后果？）
> 6. 质疑最初的问题（我当初为什么这样想？我是对的吗？我能从推理过程中得出什么结论？）

This process stops you from relying on your gut and limits strong emotional responses. This process helps you build something that lasts.

> **中文翻译**
这个过程会阻止你依赖直觉，并限制强烈的情绪反应。它能帮助你构建经得起时间考验的东西。
>

#### “Because I Said So” or “The Five Whys”｜“因为我说了算”与“五个为什么”

Children instinctively think in first principles. Just like us, they want to *understand* what’s happening in the world. To do so, they intuitively break through the fog with a game some parents have come to hate.

> **中文翻译**
孩子会本能地用第一性原理思考。和我们一样，他们想要*理解*这个世界正在发生什么。为了做到这一点，他们凭直觉用一种游戏拨开迷雾——而有些父母已经开始讨厌这种游戏。
>

“Why?”

> **中文翻译**
“为什么？”
>

“Why?”

> **中文翻译**
“为什么？”
>

“Why?”

> **中文翻译**
“为什么？”
>

Here’s an example that has played out numerous times at my house:

> **中文翻译**
下面这个场景已经在我家上演过很多次：
>

“It’s time to brush our teeth and get ready for bed.”

> **中文翻译**
“该刷牙、准备睡觉了。”
>

“Why?”

> **中文翻译**
“为什么？”
>

“Because we need to take care of our bodies, and that means we need sleep.”

> **中文翻译**
“因为我们需要照顾好自己的身体，而这意味着我们需要睡眠。”
>

“Why do we need sleep?”

> **中文翻译**
“我们为什么需要睡觉？”
>

“Because we’d die if we never slept.”

> **中文翻译**
“因为如果我们从来不睡觉，就会死。”
>

“Why would that make us die?”

> **中文翻译**
“为什么那会让我们死掉？”
>

“I don’t know; let’s go look it up.”

> **中文翻译**
“我不知道；我们一起去查查吧。”
>

Kids are just trying to understand why adults are saying something or why they want them to do something.

> **中文翻译**
孩子只是想理解，大人为什么这样说，或者为什么希望他们做某件事。
>

The first time your kid plays this game, it’s cute, but for most teachers and parents, it eventually becomes annoying. Then the answer becomes what my mom used to tell me: “Because I said so!” (Love you, Mom.)

> **中文翻译**
孩子第一次玩这个游戏时很可爱，但对大多数老师和家长来说，它最终会变得令人厌烦。于是，答案就变成了我妈妈过去常对我说的那句话：“因为我说了算！”（爱你，妈妈。）
>

Of course, I’m not always that patient with the kids. For example, I get testy when we’re late for school, or we’ve been travelling for 12 hours, or I’m trying to fit too much into the time we have. Still, I try never to say “Because I said so.”

> **中文翻译**
当然，我对孩子并不总是那么有耐心。例如，上学快迟到时、我们已经连续旅行 12 个小时时，或者我试图在有限时间里塞进太多事情时，我都会变得烦躁。即便如此，我仍尽量不说“因为我说了算”。
>

People hate the “because I said so” response for two reasons, both of which play out in the corporate world as well. The first reason we hate the game is that we feel like it slows us down. We know what we want to accomplish, and that response creates unnecessary drag. The second reason we hate this game is that after one or two questions, we are often lost. We actually don’t know why. Confronted with our own ignorance, we resort to self-defense.

> **中文翻译**
人们讨厌“因为我说了算”这种回答有两个原因，而这两个原因也都会在企业世界中上演。我们讨厌这个游戏的第一个原因，是觉得它拖慢了我们。我们知道自己想完成什么，而这种回应制造了不必要的阻力。第二个原因是，追问一两次之后，我们往往就迷失了。其实我们并不知道为什么。面对自己的无知，我们会转而进行自我防御。
>

I remember being in meetings and asking people why we were doing something this way or why they thought something was true. At first, there was a mild tolerance for this approach. After three “whys,” though, you often find yourself on the other end of some version of “we can take this offline.”

> **中文翻译**
我记得自己曾在会议中问人们，为什么我们要用这种方式做某件事，或者为什么他们认为某件事是真的。一开始，大家还会勉强容忍这种做法。但问到第三个“为什么”之后，你往往会听到某种版本的“这个问题我们会后再谈”。
>

Can you imagine how that would play out with Elon Musk? [Richard Feynman](https://fs.blog/intellectual-giants/richard-feynman/)? [Charlie Munger](https://fs.blog/intellectual-giants/charlie-munger/)? Musk would build a billion-dollar business to prove you wrong, Feynman would think you’re an idiot, and Munger would profit based on your inability to think through a problem.

> **中文翻译**
你能想象这种做法在埃隆·马斯克、[理查德·费曼](https://fs.blog/intellectual-giants/richard-feynman/)或[查理·芒格](https://fs.blog/intellectual-giants/charlie-munger/)面前会怎样收场吗？马斯克会建立一家价值十亿美元的企业来证明你错了，费曼会觉得你是个笨蛋，而芒格会利用你无法彻底想清楚问题这一点来获利。
>

> “Science is a way of thinking much more than it is a body of knowledge.”
>
>
> — Carl Sagan
>

> **中文翻译**
“科学与其说是一套知识体系，不如说是一种思维方式。”
>
>
> ——卡尔·萨根
>

### Examples of First Principles in Action｜第一性原理的实践案例

To better understand how first-principles reasoning works, let’s examine some examples.

> **中文翻译**
为了更好地理解第一性原理推理如何运作，让我们来看几个例子。
>

#### Elon Musk and SpaceX｜埃隆·马斯克与 SpaceX

Perhaps no one embodies first-principles thinking more than Elon Musk. He is one of the most audacious entrepreneurs the world has ever seen. My kids (in grades 3 and 2) refer to him as a real-life Tony Stark, thereby providing a convenient opportunity for me to remind them that by fourth grade, Musk was reading the Encyclopedia Britannica, not Pokémon.

> **中文翻译**
也许没有谁比埃隆·马斯克更能体现第一性原理思考。他是这个世界有史以来最大胆的企业家之一。我的孩子分别读三年级和二年级，他们把马斯克称为现实生活中的托尼·斯塔克；这正好给了我一个方便的机会提醒他们：马斯克到四年级时读的是《大英百科全书》，而不是《宝可梦》。
>

What’s most interesting about Musk is not what he thinks but *how* he thinks:

> **中文翻译**
马斯克最有意思的地方，不是他思考什么，而是他*如何*思考：
>

> I think people’s thinking process is too bound by convention or analogy to prior experiences. It’s rare that people try to think of something on a first principles basis. They’ll say, “We’ll do that because it’s always been done that way.” Or they’ll not do it because “Well, nobody’s ever done that, so it must not be good. But that’s just a ridiculous way to think. You have to build up the reasoning from the ground up—“from the first principles” is the phrase that’s used in physics. You look at the fundamentals and construct your reasoning from that, and then you see if you have a conclusion that works or doesn’t work, and it may or may not be different from what people have done in the past.[4]
>

> **中文翻译**
我认为，人们的思维过程太受惯例或过往经验类比的束缚。人们很少尝试在第一性原理的基础上思考一件事。他们会说：“我们这样做，是因为一直以来都是这样做的。”或者他们会因为“嗯，从来没有人那样做过，所以那一定不好”而拒绝去做。但这是一种荒谬的思维方式。你必须从头开始建立推理——物理学中使用的说法就是“从第一性原理出发”。你审视最基础的事实，并据此构建自己的推理，然后看看能否得到一个行得通的结论；这个结论可能与过去人们的做法不同，也可能相同。
>

His approach to [understanding reality](https://fs.blog/alignment/) is to begin with what is true, rather than relying on his intuition. The problem is that we don’t know as much as we think we do, so our intuition isn’t very good. We trick ourselves into thinking we know what’s possible and what’s not.

> **中文翻译**
他[理解现实](https://fs.blog/alignment/)的方法，是从真实的事实出发，而不是依赖直觉。问题在于，我们知道的并没有自己以为的那么多，因此我们的直觉并不太可靠。我们会欺骗自己，让自己以为知道什么可能、什么不可能。
>

Musk’s approach is quite different.

> **中文翻译**
马斯克的方法非常不同。
>
