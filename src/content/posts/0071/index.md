## 零、楔子

在计算机科学以及软件工程领域的学习和探索过程中，我个人对于第一性原理的理解即在于：

1. 某个事物是否“天生如此”。
2. 其在何种“约束”与“限制”之下成为当前的样子的。
3. 其发展成这个样子是否有其“必然性”。
4. 其必须“直面的问题是”什么。
5. 这种“约束”和“限制”当前是否发生了变化。

在学习一个完全的新事物，譬如某技术的时候，如果想在极短时间内快速了解“它能提供哪些服务”的话，那么打比方、做对比是比较感性直接的一种方法。

但是在想要深入了解事物本质的时候，打比方和做比较会建立错误的直觉从而干扰正确的理解，甚至可能带来思维理解上的误导和绕远。

简言之，所谓第一性原理，就是一个问题，“你真的理解了吗”。所谓“道听而途说，德之弃也。”

> I don’t know what’s the matter with people: they don’t learn by understanding; they learn by some other way—by rote or something. Their knowledge is so fragile!

下面是对各种第一性原理的资料文摘，总结自GPT 5.6 && Kimi K3。

## 一、百科定义

**1. Wikipedia: "First principle"**

> "A first principle is a basic, foundational proposition or assumption that cannot be deduced from any other proposition or assumption. In physics and other sciences, theoretical work is said to be from first principles, or ab initio, if it starts directly at the level of established science and does not make assumptions such as empirical model and fitting parameters."

**中译**：第一性原理是一个基本的、基础性的命题或假设，它无法从任何其他命题或假设中推导出来。在物理学及其他科学中，如果理论工作直接立足于成熟的科学定律层面，而不引入经验模型、拟合参数之类的假设，就称为“从第一性原理出发”，即 ab initio（从头算起）。

**链接**：[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_principle)

## 二、哲学原典

**2. 亚里士多德《物理学》卷一第1章（184a10–16）——“第一性原理”方法论的总纲**

> "When the objects of an inquiry, in any department, have principles, conditions, or elements, it is through acquaintance with these that knowledge—that is to say scientific knowledge—is attained. For we do not think that we know a thing until we are acquainted with its primary conditions or first principles, and have carried our analysis as far as its simplest elements."

**中译**：在任何一个研究领域，只要其对象存在原理、条件或元素，知识——也就是科学知识——就是通过通晓这些而获得的。因为直到我们把握了事物的最初条件或第一性原理、并把分析推进到最简单的元素时，我们才认为自己真正认识了它。

**链接**：[http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/physics.1.i.html](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/physics.1.i.html)

**3. 亚里士多德《后分析篇》卷一第3章（72b）——第一性原理本身不可证明**

> "Our own doctrine is that not all knowledge is demonstrative: on the contrary, knowledge of the immediate premises is independent of demonstration."

**中译**：我们的主张是：并非一切知识都是可证明的；恰恰相反，关于那些直接前提的知识是不依赖于证明的。（证明必须从在先的前提出发，而回溯不能无穷进行，所以最终的前提必然是不可证明的——这正是“第一”的含义。）

**链接**：[http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/posterior.1.i.html](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/posterior.1.i.html)

**4. 亚里士多德《形而上学》卷五（Δ）第1章（1013a14–15）——对“原理”（archē）一词的定义**

> "…the first basis from which a thing is known."

**中译**：（所谓第一性原理，即）“认识一个事物时最先依凭的那个基础”。希腊原文是 ἀρχή（archē），兼有“开端、本源、原理”之意。

**链接**：[http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.5.v.html](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.5.v.html)

**5. 亚里士多德《形而上学》卷一（A）第1–2章——求知与“智慧”的定义**

> "All men by nature desire to know."
> "…wisdom is knowledge about certain principles and causes."

**中译**：“求知是人的本性。”“……智慧是关于某些原理与原因的知识。”这两句是《形而上学》的开篇与题眼：亚里士多德把最高的“智慧之学”就定义为研究第一性原理与第一原因的学问。

**链接**：[http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.1.i.html](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.1.i.html)

**6. 笛卡尔《哲学原理》（Principia Philosophiae, 1644）第一部分第7条——近代哲学版的“第一性原理”**

> "…this piece of knowledge—I am thinking, therefore I exist—is the first and most certain of all to occur to anyone who philosophizes in an orderly way."

**中译**：……这条知识——“我思，故我在”——是任何按正确次序进行哲学思考的人所能获得的全部知识中，第一条、也是最确定的一条。（笛卡尔的做法是先把一切可怀疑的东西全部悬置，直到剩下不可再怀疑的基点，再从它重建整个知识体系——这正是第一性原理思维在哲学上的原型。）

**链接**：[https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-epistemology/](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-epistemology/) （斯坦福哲学百科“笛卡尔认识论”条目，其中讨论了这一条）

**7. 赫伯特·斯宾塞《第一原理》（First Principles, 1862）——书名就叫《第一性原理》的哲学著作**

斯宾塞在出版计划中这样说明本书主旨：

> "Laws of the Knowable.—A statement of the ultimate principles discernible throughout all manifestations of the Absolute—those highest generalizations now being disclosed by Science which are severally true not of one class of phenomena but of all classes of phenomena; and which are thus the keys to all classes of phenomena."

**中译**：“可知的规律”——陈述贯穿“绝对者”一切表现的终极原理，即科学正在揭示的那些最高概括：它们不是只对某一类现象为真，而是对一切现象都为真，因此是打开一切现象之门的钥匙。（注意：斯宾塞这里把“第一性原理”理解为“对一切领域都成立的最高概括”，与亚里士多德“不可再分的起点”是不同的用法，正好可以对照。）

**链接**：[https://www.gutenberg.org/files/55046/55046-h/55046-h.htm](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/55046/55046-h/55046-h.htm) （古登堡计划全文）

## 三、商界人物的著名说法

**10. 埃隆·马斯克：Kevin Rose 访谈（Foundation 20，2012）——流传最广的那段定义**

> "I think it's important to reason from first principles rather than by analogy. So the normal way we conduct our lives is, we reason by analogy. We are doing this because it's like something else that was done, or it is like what other people are doing… with slight iterations on a theme. And it's… mentally easier to reason by analogy rather than from first principles. First principles is kind of a physics way of looking at the world, and what that really means is, you… boil things down to the most fundamental truths and say, 'okay, what are we sure is true?'… and then reason up from there. That takes a lot more mental energy."

**中译**：我认为从第一性原理出发推理、而不是靠类比推理，这很重要。我们通常的生活方式是靠类比推理：这么做是因为别人做过的某件事与此类似，或因为别人都这么做……只是在一个主题上做点小改动。而且类比推理在脑力上比第一性原理省力得多。第一性原理是一种物理学式的看世界的方式，它真正的意思是：把事情煮沸到最基本的真相，问自己“好，我们确信为真的东西是什么？”……然后从这里向上推导。这要耗费多得多的脑力。

同一次访谈中的电池案例：

> "Somebody could say—and in fact people do—that battery packs are really expensive and that's just the way they will always be… they would say, 'historically, it costs $600 per kilowatt-hour'… So the first principles would be… what are the material constituents of the batteries? What is the spot market value of the material constituents?… It's got cobalt, nickel, aluminum, carbon, and some polymers for separation, and a steel can. So break that down on a material basis; if we bought that on a London Metal Exchange, what would each of these things cost? Oh, jeez, it's…$80 per kilowatt-hour. So, clearly, you just need to think of clever ways to take those materials and combine them into the shape of a battery cell, and you can have batteries that are much, much cheaper than anyone realizes."

**中译**：有人会说——事实上确实有人说——电池组就是贵，而且会一直贵下去，因为过去一直如此……他们会说“历史上每千瓦时要600美元”……那么第一性原理的问法是：电池由哪些材料构成？这些材料的现货市场价是多少？……无非是钴、镍、铝、碳、一些隔膜聚合物和一个钢壳。按材料拆开算，如果我们去伦敦金属交易所买这些原料，各值多少钱？算下来约每千瓦时80美元。所以很显然，你只需要想出聪明的办法，把这些材料组合成电芯的形状，就能造出比所有人意识到的便宜得多的电池。

**链接**：访谈视频 [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L-s_3b5fRd8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L-s_3b5fRd8) ；逐字引文与出处标注见 [https://fs.blog/first-principles/](https://fs.blog/first-principles/)

**11. 马斯克：Tim Urban《The Cook and the Chef: Musk's Secret Sauce》（Wait But Why, 2015）所引**

> "I think generally people’s thinking process is too bound by convention or analogy to prior experiences. It’s rare that people try to think of something on a first principles basis. They’ll say, “We’ll do that because it’s always been done that way.” Or they’ll not do it because “Well, nobody’s ever done that, so it must not be good.” But that’s just a ridiculous way to think. You have to build up the reasoning from the ground up—“from the first principles” is the phrase that’s used in physics. You look at the fundamentals and construct your reasoning from that, and then you see if you have a conclusion that works or doesn’t work, and it may or may not be different from what people have done in the past."
>
> A scientist gathers together only what he or she knows to be true—the first principles—and uses those as the puzzle pieces with which to construct a conclusion.

**中译**：我认为人们的思考过程太受惯例和过往经验的类比束缚了。很少有人真的尝试从第一性原理的基础上想问题……你必须从地基开始把推理一步步建起来——物理学里用的说法就是“从第一性原理出发”。你盯着那些最根本的东西，据此构建你的推理，然后看你得出的结论是否成立；它可能与人们过去的做法一致，也可能不一致。

科学家只会收集那些被认定为真实的知识——即基本原理——然后将这些知识作为拼图块，从而构建出自己的结论。

**链接**：[https://waitbutwhy.com/2015/11/the-cook-and-the-chef-musks-secret-sauce.html](https://waitbutwhy.com/2015/11/the-cook-and-the-chef-musks-secret-sauce.html)

**12. 马斯克：Wired 杂志专访（2012）——火箭原材料 2% 的出处**

> "I tend to approach things from a physics framework… Physics teaches you to reason from first principles rather than by analogy. So I said, okay, let's look at the first principles. What is a rocket made of? Aerospace-grade aluminum alloys, plus some titanium, copper, and carbon fiber. Then I asked, what is the value of those materials on the commodity market? It turned out that the materials cost of a rocket was around two percent of the typical price."

**中译**：我倾向于用物理学的框架来处理问题……物理学教会你从第一性原理而不是类比出发推理。于是我说：好，我们看第一性原理。火箭是由什么构成的？航空级铝合金，加上一些钛、铜和碳纤维。然后我问：这些材料在大宗商品市场上值多少钱？结果发现，火箭的材料成本大约只有火箭典型售价的百分之二。

**链接**：[https://www.wired.com/2012/10/ff-elon-musk-qa](https://www.wired.com/2012/10/ff-elon-musk-qa)

**13. 拉里·佩奇谈马斯克（引自 Ashlee Vance 的马斯克传记）**

> "What are the physics of it? How much time will it take? How much will it cost? How much cheaper can I make it? There's this level of engineering and physics that you need to make judgments about what's possible and interesting. Elon is unusual in that he knows that, and he also knows business and organization and leadership and governmental issues."

**中译**：（他的思路是）这件事的物理原理是什么？要花多少时间？成本多少？还能便宜多少？要判断什么事是可能且有意思的，就需要这种工程与物理层面的判断力。埃隆的不寻常之处在于，他既懂这个层面，又懂商业、组织、领导力和政府事务。

**链接**：[https://fs.blog/first-principles/](https://fs.blog/first-principles/) （文中引自 Ashlee Vance《Elon Musk: Tesla, SpaceX, and the Quest for a Fantastic Future》第354页）

**14. 查理·芒格：南加州大学商学院演讲《A Lesson on Elementary, Worldly Wisdom》（1994）**

> "What is elementary, worldly wisdom? Well, the first rule is that you can't really know anything if you just remember isolated facts and try and bang 'em back. If the facts don't hang together on a latticework of theory, you don't have them in a usable form. You've got to have models in your head. And you've got to array your experience—both vicarious and direct—on this latticework of models."

**中译**：什么是基本的、世俗的智慧？第一条规则是：如果你只是记住一些孤立的事实并试图把它们背出来，你不可能真正懂得任何东西。如果这些事实没有悬挂在一个理论的格栅框架上，它们就不是可用的形态。你必须在头脑中拥有模型，并且把你的经验——无论是间接得来的还是亲身经历的——挂在这个模型的格栅上。（这就是芒格“多元思维模型格栅”的出处；与第一性原理的关系在于：他要求你把知识挂到各学科最基础的原理上，而不是堆事实。后收入《穷查理宝典》。）

**链接**：[https://github.com/remidinishanth/awesome-charlie-munger](https://github.com/remidinishanth/awesome-charlie-munger) （收录该演讲 PDF 记录稿；印刷版见《Poor Charlie's Almanack》）

## 四、知名文章

**15. Shane Parrish（Farnam Street）《First Principles: The Building Blocks of True Knowledge》**

> "…it's a tool to help break down complicated problems by separating what we know is absolutely true from anything that is an assumption. What remains are the essentials. If you know the first principles of something, you can build the rest of your knowledge around them to produce something new."
> "…everything that is not a law of nature is just a shared belief."

**中译**：“……它是一个工具，帮你拆解复杂问题：把你确知绝对为真的东西与一切假设分离开，剩下的就是本质要素。如果你知道了某件事的第一性原理，你就可以围绕它们建构其余的知识，创造出新的东西。”“……凡不是自然法则的东西，都只是一种共同信念。”（这篇是当代传播第一性原理思维影响最大的长文，还给了两个实操工具：苏格拉底式提问和“五个为什么”。）

**16. James Clear《First Principles: Elon Musk on the Power of Thinking for Yourself》**

> "First principles thinking is the act of boiling a process down to the fundamental parts that you know are true and building up from there."
> "A first principle is a basic assumption that cannot be deduced any further."

**中译**：“第一性原理思维，就是把一个过程提炼到你确知为真的最基本部分，然后从这里向上构建。”“第一性原理是一个无法再被进一步推导的基本假设。”（这篇文章以旅行箱轮子在1970年才被发明为例，说明“优化形式 vs. 优化功能”的区别，是第一性原理很好的入门读物。）
